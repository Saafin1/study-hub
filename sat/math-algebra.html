Algebra

~35% of the Math section (13–15 of 44 questions). Covers linear equations, linear functions, systems of two linear equations, and linear inequalities — anything where the variable has no exponent.

How to recognize this question type

  • The equation(s) only have x (or another variable) to the first power — no x², no x under a square root, no x in a denominator.
  • Word problems that describe a constant rate plus a flat starting amount ("$20 plus $0.10 per text") are linear — they translate to y = mx + b.
  • Two equations given together, asking for a value that works in both → system of equations.
  • Phrases like "at least," "at most," "more than," "no more than" → linear inequality.

Fast elimination strategy

  • Plug the answer choices back in. On multiple choice, if solving feels slow, plug each choice into the original equation and see which one makes it true — often faster than solving from scratch.
  • Check the sign first. A huge fraction of wrong answers are the right number with the wrong sign, or flipped (like 1/3 instead of 3). Eliminate any answer that's clearly the "flipped" version of another.
  • Estimate before calculating. If a word problem describes something growing, the answer should be positive and reasonable in size — eliminate choices that are negative or absurdly large/small.

Solve it on Desmos

You get a graphing calculator on the real Digital SAT (built into the testing app, Desmos-powered) — and it's built into this page below too, so you can use it exactly like on test day.

  • To solve an equation like 4x − 7 = 2x + 9: type y_1=4x-7 on one line and y_2=2x+9 on the next. Desmos draws both lines and shows a point where they cross — click that point, and its x-value is your answer.
  • To evaluate a function like f(4) when f(x) = 3x − 5: type f(x)=3x-5, then on the next line just type f(4) — Desmos computes the value instantly.
  • To solve a system of two equations: type both equations in (solved for y if needed) and click the intersection point Desmos shows — that point's coordinates are your solution.
  • To check an inequality: graph both sides as if they were equal, find where they cross, then reason about which side of that point satisfies the inequality.

Practice questions

1. Solve for x: 4x − 7 = 2x + 9

Recognize it

Variable on both sides, no exponents → a straightforward linear equation.

Solve algebraically

Subtract 2x from both sides: 2x − 7 = 9. Add 7 to both sides: 2x = 16. Divide by 2: x = 8.

Solve on Desmos

Type y_1=4x-7 and y_2=2x+9. The lines cross at x = 8.

Why the other choices are wrong

A) 4 comes from dividing 16 by 4 instead of 2. C) −8 comes from a sign slip when moving terms across the equals sign. D) 16 comes from forgetting the final division step.

2. If f(x) = 3x − 5, what is f(4)?

Recognize it

"f(4)" means: plug 4 in for every x in the function.

Solve algebraically

f(4) = 3(4) − 5 = 12 − 5 = 7.

Solve on Desmos

Type f(x)=3x-5, then on the next line type f(4) — it evaluates to 7 automatically.

Why the other choices are wrong

B) 12 forgets to subtract 5. C) −7 comes from a sign error. D) 17 mistakenly adds instead of subtracts.

3. If 2x + y = 10 and x − y = 2, what is the value of x + y?

Recognize it

Two linear equations, two unknowns → system of equations. You need to solve for both x and y first.

Solve algebraically

Add the two equations: (2x + y) + (x − y) = 10 + 2 → 3x = 12 → x = 4. Plug into the second equation: 4 − y = 2 → y = 2. So x + y = 4 + 2 = 6.

Solve on Desmos

Type y=10-2x and y=x-2. Click the intersection point: (4, 2). Add the coordinates: 4 + 2 = 6.

Why the other choices are wrong

B) 8 and D) 12 come from arithmetic slips when combining the equations. C) 2 is just the value of y alone, not x + y.

4. A line passes through the points (1, 2) and (3, 8). What is the slope of the line?

Recognize it

Two points given, asking for slope → use the slope formula: (change in y) ÷ (change in x).

Solve algebraically

Slope = (8 − 2) / (3 − 1) = 6 / 2 = 3.

Solve on Desmos

Just type the arithmetic directly: (8-2)/(3-1) — Desmos computes 3 instantly. You can also plot both points to visually confirm the line rises steeply.

Why the other choices are wrong

B) 1/3 is the slope flipped upside down (a very common mistake — always double check which difference is on top). C) 6 forgets to divide. D) 2 comes from dividing the wrong pair of numbers.

5. Solve the inequality: 2x + 3 < 11

Recognize it

Solve inequalities exactly like equations, with one rule: flip the inequality sign only if you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number (not needed here).

Solve algebraically

Subtract 3 from both sides: 2x < 8. Divide by 2 (positive, so the sign stays the same): x < 4.

Solve on Desmos

Graph y=2x+3 and y=11. They cross at x = 4. Since the coefficient on x is positive and we need the left side to be smaller, the answer is everything to the left of that point: x < 4.

Why the other choices are wrong

B) 7 forgets to divide by 2. C) has the right number but the wrong direction — a sign-flip mistake. D) 8 forgets the division step entirely.

6. A phone plan charges a flat fee of $20 per month plus $0.10 for each text message sent. Which equation gives the total monthly cost, y, for x text messages?

Recognize it

A flat starting fee plus a constant cost per item is the classic y = mx + b setup: b is the flat fee (y-intercept), m is the per-unit rate (slope).

Solve algebraically

Flat fee = $20 → that's b. Cost per text = $0.10 → that's m, multiplied by the number of texts, x. So y = 0.10x + 20, same as y = 20 + 0.10x.

Solve on Desmos

Not needed for translating word problems — but once you have the equation, you could graph it and check that y = 20 when x = 0 (matches "$20 flat fee with no texts sent").

Why the other choices are wrong

B) and C) swap which number is the rate and which is the flat fee. D) subtracts instead of adds, which would make the cost negative at zero texts — that doesn't match a flat monthly fee.

Algebra (~35%, ~13-15 questions)

This domain covers linear equations, linear functions, and systems of two linear equations. "Linear" just means no exponents on the variable — every term is a plain number or a number times a variable.

Worked example: Solve for x: 3x + 5 = 2x − 7
  1. Get the x-terms on one side: subtract 2x from both sides → x + 5 = −7
  2. Subtract 5 from both sides → x = −12
  3. Check: 3(−12) + 5 = −31, and 2(−12) − 7 = −31. They match, so x = −12 is correct.
Tip: Whatever you do to one side of an equation, do to the other. Always check your answer by plugging it back in — it takes 10 seconds and catches almost every arithmetic mistake.

Advanced Math (~35%, ~13-15 questions)

This covers quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, and radical equations, plus nonlinear functions. The most common question type is solving quadratics (equations with an x² term).

Worked example: Solve for x: x² − 5x + 6 = 0
  1. Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5: those are −2 and −3.
  2. Factor: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0
  3. Either factor can be zero, so x = 2 or x = 3.
  4. Check: (2)² − 5(2) + 6 = 4 − 10 + 6 = 0. Correct.
Tip: If a quadratic doesn't factor easily, the quadratic formula always works: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a, using the equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0.

Problem-Solving & Data Analysis (~15%, ~5-7 questions)

This domain covers ratios, rates, percentages, and interpreting data from tables, graphs, and scatterplots — plus basic statistics like mean, median, and mode.

Worked example: A shirt originally costs $40 and is on sale for 25% off. What is the sale price?
  1. 25% off means you pay 75% of the original price.
  2. 75% as a decimal is 0.75.
  3. $40 × 0.75 = $30.
Tip: For "percent off" problems, it's faster to multiply by what you keep (75%) than to find the discount and subtract it in a separate step.

Geometry & Trigonometry (~15%, ~5-7 questions)

This covers area, volume, lines, angles, triangles, circles, and right-triangle trigonometry (sine, cosine, tangent).

Worked example: A right triangle has legs of length 3 and 4. What is the sine of the angle opposite the side of length 3?
  1. First find the hypotenuse using the Pythagorean theorem: 3² + 4² = c² → 9 + 16 = 25 → c = 5.
  2. Sine = opposite ÷ hypotenuse.
  3. The side of length 3 is opposite the angle we care about, so sin = 3/5 = 0.6.
Tip: Remember SOH-CAH-TOA: Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent.
